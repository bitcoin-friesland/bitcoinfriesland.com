# What is Bitcoin and how does it work? A beginner's guide

Bitcoin is digital money that belongs to no one and that nobody can print more of. Here is how it works in plain language, without jargon.

- **Money without a bank.** With bitcoin you send money directly to someone else over the internet. There is no bank in between. Thousands of computers around the world keep track together of who owns what.
- **Never more than 21 million.** There will never be more than 21 million bitcoin. Nobody can change that number or print extra. That is why people call it digital gold.
- **You don’t buy a whole bitcoin.** 1 bitcoin is made of 100 million small pieces called sats. So you can start with a few euros, just like paying with cents.
- **Your keys, your money.** You keep bitcoin in a wallet: an app on your phone. It gives you a secret recovery phrase of 12 or 24 words. Whoever has that phrase has your bitcoin. Write it on paper and never share it.
- **Fast payments with Lightning.** For small amounts you use Lightning. You pay in a few seconds for almost nothing, for example at Frisian businesses on the Bitcoin map.
- **The price moves a lot.** What a bitcoin is worth in euros goes up and down a lot. Never put in money you cannot afford to lose. This is an explanation, not financial advice.

## How does Bitcoin work?

1. **You open a wallet.** An app on your phone. It creates keys that let only you spend your bitcoin.
2. **You send a payment.** Scan a QR code or paste an address, pick an amount and confirm. Like a bank transfer, but without the bank.
3. **The network checks it.** Thousands of computers (nodes) check that you really own the bitcoin and are not spending it twice.
4. **Miners record it.** About every ten minutes a new block of payments is added to the blockchain. After that, your payment is final.

## How many bitcoin are there?

There will never be more than 21 million bitcoin. That is fixed in the rules every computer in the network follows.

New bitcoin are created as a reward for miners: 3.125 bitcoin per block since April 2024. Roughly every four years that reward is cut in half (the halving). The next one is around 2028, and the last bitcoin arrives around the year 2140.

In November 2026 just over 20.1 million bitcoin were in circulation: almost 96 percent of all there will ever be.

## Words you will hear a lot

- **Wallet:** The app where you keep and send bitcoin.
- **Sats:** The smallest piece of bitcoin. 100 million sats make 1 bitcoin.
- **Recovery phrase:** 12 or 24 words that restore your wallet if you lose your phone. The most important thing you own.
- **Blockchain:** The public ledger of all bitcoin payments, kept by computers around the world.
- **Lightning:** A fast layer on top of bitcoin for small, cheap payments.
- **Exchange:** A company where you swap euros for bitcoin and back.

## Who invented Bitcoin?

Bitcoin started in 2009. Its creator used the name Satoshi Nakamoto; nobody knows who that really is. Since then the network has run day and night, without a boss.

## Frequently asked questions

- **Who is in charge of Bitcoin?** Nobody. No company, bank or government runs Bitcoin. Thousands of computers around the world run the same rules, and nobody can change them alone.
- **How do I buy bitcoin?** You exchange euros for bitcoin at an exchange or broker. Start small and then send your bitcoin to your own wallet. The page for consumers has a step-by-step plan.
- **Where can I pay with bitcoin in Friesland?** The Bitcoin map lists more than 40 Frisian businesses that accept Bitcoin or Lightning, from bakery to pedicure.
